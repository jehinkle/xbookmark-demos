Adaptive trial designs gain ground in early oncology

By Maria Okafor · October 28, 2026 · 6 min read

Drug developers running first-in-human cancer studies are increasingly replacing the traditional 3+3 dose-escalation scheme with model-based designs, according to a review of trial registrations published this month.

The shift matters because the 3+3 rule, which escalates dose after cohorts of three patients, tends to treat many participants at doses that are either too low to work or too toxic to tolerate. Bayesian approaches such as the Bayesian optimal interval (BOIN) design and the continual reassessment method (CRM) use every patient's outcome to update an estimate of the maximum tolerated dose.

Faster decisions, fewer patients at sub-therapeutic doses

"We see sponsors reach a recommended phase 2 dose two or three cohorts sooner," said one biostatistician who reviewed the registrations. Regulators have signaled openness: FDA's Project Optimus encourages randomized dose comparisons before pivotal trials.

Model-based designs are no longer exotic. They are becoming the default for sponsors who want to answer the dose question properly.

Critics note that model-based methods demand more statistical support during the trial and careful simulation before it starts, and that dose-escalation meetings must be scheduled around fast data turnaround.
